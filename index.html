Supplier Agreement Review: Contractual Breaches & Termination Strategy

TO:Chief Executive Officer & Board Risk Committee
FROM:JHC Senior Advisory Team
DATE:June 5, 2026
RE:Material Breach Evaluation of Core Logistics Provider Contract

1. Executive Summary

This memorandum evaluates our current legal and operational standing regarding the persistent performance failures of our primary logistics provider. Over the past three quarters, the provider has consistently failed to meet service level agreements (SLAs), causing operational disruption.

The core question is whether the company should immediately exercise its right of termination. Based on rigorous structural analysis of the agreement, we conclude that . This position minimizes financial exposure while maintaining programmatic stability during vendor transition.

2. Facts

On October 12, 2024, the company entered into a Master Service Agreement (MSA) with the provider for continental freight and warehouse routing.

Our operational records demonstrate that starting in Q3 2025, delivery delays exceeded the acceptable 2% margin. By Q1 2026, the error rate in critical routing tasks reached . Formal cure notices were issued on February 14 and March 28, 2026. No substantial performance remediation has been observed despite written assurances from the provider's executive team.

3. Analysis

The legal threshold for contractual termination rests entirely on the definition of a "Material Breach" within the contract framework. The provider argues that delays stem from macroeconomic constraints and do not reflect intentional non-performance.

However, an evaluation of the operational dependencies reveals that the continuity of our supply chain is deeply integrated with their routing software. Under standard commercial law interpretations, when a counterparty fails to maintain the foundational infrastructure required for execution, the contract's core purpose is frustrated. Therefore, the accumulative impact of these minor operational lapses transforms them into a single, comprehensive failure of consideration.

4. Risks

While legal termination is justified, it introduces clear operational and retaliatory risks that the board must consider. First, an immediate exit could prompt the provider to suspend all active tracking nodes, blinding our logistics managers during a transition phase.

Second, there is a moderate risk of counter-litigation. The provider may claim that our strict adherence to the SLA metrics constitutes an unreasonable application of the penalty clauses, arguing bad faith. However, our meticulously archived communications and formal cure notices provide an airtight defense against such assertions.

5. Recommendation

We advise the board to authorize a phased termination strategy rather than an abrupt shutdown. This approach mitigates operational gaps while establishing an unassailable legal posture.

The company should issue a formal Notice of Intent to Terminate by the close of this business week, citing the un-cured material breaches under Section 8.2 of the MSA. Concurrently, the transition team should begin migrating database nodes to the backup provider, ensuring a clean cutover within 30 days.
